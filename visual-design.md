# Design with intent

A useful product should also feel considered. Choose a direction that fits your users and their work—not a default dashboard with different labels.

During Explore and your existing individual prototype exercise:

- Ask Codex to research relevant industry interaction conventions and 2–3 strong examples of your product type. Look at the working product, not only its marketing homepage. What makes the important task clear? What should feel familiar?
- Pick a few principles worth borrowing: information hierarchy, composition, typography, data density, navigation, or purposeful interaction. Explain why they suit your users. Use references as inspiration, not something to copy wholesale.
- Compare genuinely different compositions in your individual prototypes, not recolors of the same layout. Agree on a coherent visual direction before building the shared app.

During Build and Iterate, have Codex inspect the actual rendered interface. Refine spacing, alignment, hierarchy, readable contrast and consistency. Check keyboard focus, small screens, long content, and empty/loading/error states. If motion helps explain a change, keep it restrained and honor reduced-motion preferences; decoration must not hide the work or slow it down.

## A few places to begin—not a required aesthetic

- [Chatham Financial](https://www.chathamfinancial.com/): brand character, typography and editorial hierarchy.
- [Linear](https://linear.app/): workflow product composition and clear interaction hierarchy.
- [Stripe](https://stripe.com/): deliberate typography and visual storytelling; distinguish marketing layouts from working tools.
- [W3C ARIA Authoring Practices](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/): familiar, accessible interaction patterns. Use appropriate native controls before inventing new ones.

Find other examples that fit your particular product. Keep a couple of links and the reasoning with your existing project decisions—no extra presentation or design deliverable is required. The hub illustrations suggest possibilities, not mandatory features, layouts or animation.

Follow the current Chatham Vibes guidance for approved fonts, assets, libraries and runtime boundaries. Your team still owns the product and acceptance decisions.
