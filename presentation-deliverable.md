# Present your work

Reserve about 20 minutes within the two-hour lab to prepare and rehearse, even if the app is unfinished. Present for **5–7 minutes**, with roughly **4–5 minutes showing the app**. Use three slides:

| Slide | Purpose |
|---|---|
| 1 — Problem and app | Briefly introduce who it helps, the problem and what you built. About 30–45 seconds. |
| 2 — Show the app | Switch into the app and demonstrate a realistic user journey, your favorite useful feature and important limits. About 4–5 minutes. Keep a screenshot here as a backup. |
| 3 — Written Q1–Q6 responses | Answer all six questions below for facilitator review. You do not need to present this slide or read the answers aloud. Use concise answers; put supporting detail in speaker notes. |

## Final-slide questions

1. **Q1 — Problem and app:** Whose problem did you solve, what does the app do, and what is an important limit?
2. **Q2 — AI and people:** What did AI do, and which decisions or checks did your team own? Distinguish AI used to build the app from AI used inside it when relevant.
3. **Q3 — Assumption:** Which assumption did you investigate, what did you learn, and what changed or was confirmed?
4. **Q4 — Verification:** What did you check, what happened, and what remains unverified?
5. **Q5 — Failure and response:** What failed—or what failure did you deliberately test—and how did you respond and recheck?
6. **Q6 — Improve the process:** What would improve your AI process next time, and how would you assess its effect?

Read [ai-tooling-access.md](ai-tooling-access.md), then read Chatham's deck-builder SKILL.md from the local ai-tooling folder. Follow its design and validation guidance; no plugin installation is needed. Use actual decisions, sources and check results. Do not invent incidents, time savings, ROI or successful tests. Label simulations and unfinished features. Render and inspect the slides; keep the answers readable instead of shrinking text to fit. No extra agenda or architecture slide is required.

## Submit one package per team

Use the main [Build Lab hub](https://www.chathamvibes.com/vibe_apps/773/render), under your finalized team assignment:

1. Save your team's Vibes app link. In the submission form, refresh if you just changed it and identify the version demonstrated (or say it was not recorded).
2. Ask Codex to prepare the full project submission ZIP using [submission-package.md](submission-package.md), then choose **Load submission ZIP**. Review the loaded presentation, project archive, log and app version.
3. Alternatively, choose the files individually: **editable PowerPoint (.pptx, up to 50 MiB)**, **project source archive (.zip, up to 50 MiB)** and **project log/feedback (.md or .txt, up to 100,000 characters)**. Include the written final slide; prepare the log using [project-review-record.md](project-review-record.md).
4. Confirm the review and submit. Keep the receipt/revision and local files. A selected or uploaded file alone is not a confirmed submission.

Any team member can submit a revised package while submissions are open. Previous revisions are retained; admins review the latest package. Do not add your submission to shared starter resources. The log is stored behind team/facilitator checks. Presentations and project source archives use app-scoped document storage: exclude confidential feedback and private client data, and keep sensitive reflection in the log. Never include credentials or unnecessary personal data.

If uploading fails, keep your files and email the same ZIP (PowerPoint, project source, app link/version and log) to your facilitator using the address they provide. Do not guess an address or send automatically. No duplicate email is needed after a confirmed hub submission. If the outcome is uncertain, refresh and check the receipt before retrying.

The existing team card is a working aid. Codex should reuse its notes to prepare the final slide and log, not ask you to fill in duplicate reports. Optional backup screenshots can stay in the deck; a recording is not required.
